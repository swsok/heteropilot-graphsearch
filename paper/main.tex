\begin{abstract}
%% Every sentence here traces to a row of paper/CLAIMS.md. The hardware
%% sentence is deliberately the weakest one in the abstract, because the
%% strongest hardware result available is a failure.
Serving a large language model on a heterogeneous cluster means placing roles
on accelerators. Planners that enumerate
configurations over groups of interchangeable devices treat two placements of
one configuration as one candidate, yet on hardware the placement can decide
whether a service meets its latency target. We enumerate placements and fold
two only when a graph isomorphism confirms their candidate graphs agree; the
graphs carry the shared communication boundary, and removing it mis-merges the
counterexample pair the relation exists for. Elimination is a relaxation of the
feasibility test that publishes the optimism it assumed, and the bounded
evaluation budget reports what it never reached. In every experiment with an
exhaustive oracle, no feasible placement the evaluator
judged was wrongly eliminated and nothing differently priced was folded; under
the simulator the compression saved more time than it cost. On hardware, one
plan at two placements of itself has a median $p99$ time to first token
\egfiveTtwoOverTone{}x apart at the load knee, with non-overlapping ranges. Which resource is shared
must be measured: a declaration we found wrong on one bus held on an
inter-node NIC, where a disaggregated deployment nevertheless slowed far less
than predicted; an optimistic throughput bound, tested on one rejected
candidate, sits an order of magnitude above delivered capacity.
\end{abstract}
