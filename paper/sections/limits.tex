\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

Some of these were stated before the experiment that could have revealed
them: the asymmetric precondition and the scope deferrals are the research
design's own failure conditions and scope. Others were found by running the experiments---the causes
of the simulator's failures, the adapter's first-pair figure, the size of the
inter-node contention effect---and each is recorded with the decision that
found it. Neither kind is argued away.

% claim: C4
\paragraph{The compression has a precondition, and clusters can fail it.}
Exact equivalence folds placements only where the cluster has repeated
structure. On the toy asymmetric fixture, where no two placements are
isomorphic, it folds nothing: the ratio is \egoneRatioAsym. The synthetic
scaling clusters still fold at their asymmetric end: at 128 devices the ratio
runs from \egsixRatioAsym{} there to \egsixRatioSym{} at full symmetry. The asymmetric configurations are in the corpus precisely so
the number is reported. Where the isomorphism check costs more than the simulation
it saves, the registered response is to demote exact compression to a cache key
rather than to argue the cost away.

% claim: C13
% claim: C14
\paragraph{A share of placements never simulate, and on one fixture it is a fifth of them.}
On the smallest corpus \egthreeUnjudgedLab{} of \egthreeEmbeddingsLab{} placements returned no verdict.
% claim: C14
They are reported as \texttt{unknown\_measurement} and are not counted as
infeasible, so every correctness result in this paper is a statement about the
placements the simulator judged. The cause is two causes---a missing profile
for a parallelism degree, and a decode instance exhausting its KV---and neither
becomes a verdict by being understood. The second is the more interesting: the
evaluator detects that the run cannot proceed and raises, so the failure
carries a reason but not a verdict, and it is reported exactly as a simulator
error of any other kind is.

% claim: C15
\paragraph{The contention model is fluid, and has an upper message size.}
It is processor sharing over declared shared resources, recomputed when a flow
starts or finishes. It is not packet-level; it does not model contention
\emph{between} candidates, which are alternatives rather than co-tenants; and
above a message size the hardware overlaps the two directions of one link in a
way processor sharing does not capture, making the model pessimistic there: it
predicts those transfers about a third slower than measured. That boundary is stated rather than absorbed into a fitted
capacity.

% claim: C34
\paragraph{The simulator is told a placement's bandwidth, not what it shares.}
For each placement the adapter replaces the simulator's link figure with that
placement's own: for a tensor-parallel group, the minimum over every rank pair
of that pair's best path, each link given its measured all-reduce bus bandwidth
at the group's own number of ranks where the cluster description carries one,
and its edge capacity otherwise, capped in either case by what its shared
resource's reservations leave. That is the corrected adapter. The registered
hardware rows were all predicted by the earlier one, which read the group's
first rank pair at its edge capacity; Section~\ref{sec:eval-hw} reports what
re-predicting them changes, post hoc. What the adapter cannot pass on is which
\emph{shared resource} a flow crosses, so contention between a candidate's own
flows on one port or NIC is invisible to the evaluator. The adapter's report
names the shared resources it dropped, which makes that gap legible but does
not close it---and it is not a complete account of what the simulator was not
told: the rank pairs the earlier adapter ignored were never in it. Closing the
gap needs a simulator with a link graph, or a model that folds contention into
the numbers passed---and the second would make the simulator's output
unattributable, which is why it was not done.

% claim: C3
For a tensor-parallel group of more than two ranks the compression's demand
labels still read the first rank pair, as the earlier adapter did, so on such
groups the label and the evaluator shared one blind spot, and the zero
mis-merge count there is not independent evidence that the labels are
sufficient. The same first-pair read remains in the fluid contention model's
choice of a flow's path and in the ranker's cut margin; neither decides what
the simulator is told, and correcting them would change which candidates the
registered runs reached, so they are recorded rather than changed here.

% claim: C22
% claim: C30
\paragraph{Inter-node contention on a disaggregated deployment is smaller than
the model predicts.}
The contention measurements cover an intra-node bus and an inter-node NIC, and
inter-node \emph{P/D} now runs: prefill on one node, decode on the other, the
KV pulled across the NIC, every request completed. It is deployed by the
experiment's own harness, because the deployment backend this work extends
serves a single engine and has no router. Measured as a paired mean over
requests, at a load the configuration sustains, a background stream on the
same NIC lengthened the interval from prefill response to first decoded token
by \egfivePdEffect{} ms---with a standard deviation of \egfivePdSd{} ms over
three pairs, so not distinguishable from zero---where the fluid model
predicted \egfivePdPredicted{} ms: a ratio of \egfivePdRatio{}, outside the
registered agreement band. The model
enters the background as a standing reservation of its duty cycle, which slows
a transfer more than a competing flow of that duty cycle would; even so, the
hardware's effect is below what processor sharing with such a flow predicts,
and this experiment does not separate why. Deployment to the NPU in the
design's scope is impossible, because no backend for it exists.

\paragraph{Every fixture but three is fictional.}
The toy clusters and the synthetic scaling clusters are invented and say so in
their headers; a result computed from one is a result about the search, never
about hardware. The exceptions are the eight-GPU node's and the two-node
pair's descriptions, generated from the microbenchmarks' raw files with
unmeasured figures marked as placeholders, and the holdout derived from the
first by one assumed reservation, which is a topology variation rather than a
measurement. A simulation at 128 devices is not a large-scale
accuracy result and is not offered as one.

% claim: C21
% not-established
\paragraph{The knee shift is not established.}
That the slower wire moves the load knee itself, and not only the latency at
it, was observed only in the pilot runs, which are the fitted set the
specification was derived from and are excluded from validation; no results
file carries it, and the paper does not claim it.

\paragraph{What a certificate certifies.}
Where the search can certify that nothing unevaluated could have been better,
that statement is bounded by the declared candidate space and by the evaluation
model. It does not say that no better deployment exists; it says that none
exists among candidates this specification admits, judged by this evaluator.

\paragraph{Deferred by scope.}
A single tensor-parallel group spanning two accelerator vendors, and KV-cache
format conversion between them, are outside this work. Learned rankers and
mixed parallelism degrees within one group are future work. These are stated as
scope rather than as difficulty: nothing here shows they cannot be done.
