\section{Candidates and exact compression}
\label{sec:candidates}

\paragraph{Templates and embeddings.}
A \emph{template} is what HeteroPilot~\cite{heteropilot} already produces: a role
assignment with parallelism degrees and knobs, naming islands rather than
devices. An \emph{embedding} is a template placed on named devices. Replicas of
one role are interchangeable, so only one ordering of each partition into
replica groups is enumerated---the lowest remaining device fixes the next
group---and the orderings folded away are counted in closed form,
$\prod_a R_a! - 1$ per placement over the assignments $a$ with $R_a$ replicas
each; enumerating them, as an earlier version did, did not terminate on an
eight-device cluster and is kept only as the closed form's test.

\paragraph{Candidate graphs.}
Each embedding becomes a small labelled graph: a vertex per rank group carrying
its role, parallelism degree, device model, price and power, and edges carrying
the flows between them. The graph also carries the \emph{boundary}: the shared
resources the candidate's traffic crosses, each with the capacity it has left
after external reservations.

\paragraph{Folding, and why a hash is not enough.}
Two embeddings are folded only if their candidate graphs are isomorphic. A
Weisfeiler--Leman hash~\cite{wlkernel} buckets them, because comparing every
pair is quadratic; within a bucket a VF2 isomorphism check~\cite{vf2}, as
NetworkX~\cite{networkx} implements it, decides, and the hash never
decides alone, because a collision would surface as a wrong price attributed
to a candidate nobody evaluated---indistinguishable, in the output, from the
search working. The bucket key also contains HeteroPilot's prediction
key---model, serving architecture and knobs, which change a simulation but are
not in the graph---because two candidates that may share a prediction are, by
that act, claimed interchangeable.

\paragraph{The boundary is what makes the relation correct.}
Three nodes are identical in every local attribute. A prefill group on node $X$
sends to a decode group on node $Z$, and a prefill group on $Y$ sends to the
same decode group. The two placements agree in role, parallelism, device
model, price and traffic shape, and differ in one thing: $X$'s uplink already
carries traffic this planner does not control. With the boundary in the
signature they are two representatives; without it they are one, and the
evaluator's number for whichever was chosen is attributed to both.

The research design's worked example---enumeration, then folding, the runtime
compatibility check and the bound, only the last two of which judge a
candidate---is drawn in the artifact (\texttt{figures/concept\_sec9.tex}).

\paragraph{What compression is not.}
A representative's multiplicity is the number of placements it stands for, not
a concurrency limit: one standing for sixteen placements does not mean sixteen
may run at once. Contention between representatives deployed together is a
separate relation, a conflict, computed only on request because it is
quadratic in the number of embeddings.
