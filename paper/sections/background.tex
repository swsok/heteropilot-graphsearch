\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Execution islands.}
The planner this work extends describes a cluster as a set of \emph{execution
islands}: maximal groups of accelerators of one model, joined by one kind of
intra-island interconnect, that can host a single serving engine. A candidate
names an island and a parallelism degree; the deployer then resolves the island
to its device list and lets the runtime take the devices it needs.

That abstraction is already a compression, and an unstated one. Enumerating
candidates over islands rather than over device subsets collapses every
placement within an island into a single candidate, which is sound exactly when
the devices of an island are interchangeable. They are interchangeable in model
and in memory by the definition of an island. They are not necessarily
interchangeable in what their traffic crosses, and nothing in the island
definition requires them to be.

\paragraph{Approximate merging already present.}
The planner also merges performance envelopes: two configurations whose
\emph{twin} keys agree share a cached prediction. That merge is approximate by
design---it is a cache key, chosen for hit rate---and the work here does not
replace it. The distinction matters for reading the two together: an
approximate merge that is wrong costs accuracy in a prediction, while an
equivalence that is wrong costs a candidate its verdict.

\paragraph{The simulator, and what it is not told.}
Latency predictions come from a discrete-event simulator driven by an
analytical network backend. It accepts a link bandwidth and a latency per
collective, and it has no representation of a link graph: there is no way to
tell it that two groups share a wire, or that something outside the deployment
is already using part of one. Anything the search knows about shared resources
must therefore either be folded into the numbers it passes, or reported as
lost. Section~\ref{sec:adapter} takes the second route and says why.

\paragraph{What is reused and what is new.}
Section~\ref{sec:limits} carries the module-level table. In summary: the
inventory, candidate generator, predictor interface, optimiser and deployment
backends are the existing planner's and are used unmodified; five hooks were
added to it, each recorded as a numbered deviation in that repository; and the
graph representation, equivalence, elimination, ranking, adaptive budget,
simulator adapter and restoration are new. No behaviour of the existing planner
changes when the hooks are not used, which its own golden tests check.
