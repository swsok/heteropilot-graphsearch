Placement is a decision a serving planner has to make and, when it enumerates
over islands rather than devices, has no language for. This work gives it one:
an equivalence relation that folds only what a graph isomorphism confirms
interchangeable and carries the shared communication boundary, so that
removing the boundary mis-merges the counterexample pair the full relation
keeps apart. Elimination is a relaxation of the feasibility test; in every
experiment that runs an exhaustive oracle, no feasible placement the evaluator
judged was wrongly removed and nothing differently priced was folded, and on
hardware the bound was tested on one rejected candidate and was right. The
budget names what it never judged, and under the real simulator the
compression saved more time than it cost. On hardware one plan at two
placements of itself has a median $p99$ time to first token
\egfiveTtwoOverTone{}x apart at the load knee, with ranges that do not overlap;
on an inter-node disaggregated deployment the model's contention prediction is
far larger than what the hardware shows, which the registered criterion
reports as a miss.
