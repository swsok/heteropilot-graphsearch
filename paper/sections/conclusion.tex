\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% claim: C24
Placement is a decision a serving planner has to make and, when it enumerates
over islands rather than over devices, has no language for. This work gives it
one: an equivalence relation over placements that folds only what a graph
isomorphism confirms interchangeable, and that carries the shared communication
boundary so that two placements alike in every local attribute but crossing
different contended links are kept apart. Removing the boundary from the
signature produces mis-merges the full relation does not.

% claim: C2
Elimination is a relaxation of the feasibility test or it is not called
elimination, and each check publishes the optimistic assumptions it made. Across
every experiment reported here, no feasible candidate was wrongly removed and no
two differently-priced placements were folded together; both numbers are
reported for every arm rather than summarised once, because they are conditions
rather than scores.

% claim: C11
The budget is adaptive and, more importantly, honest about itself: the output
names how many representatives went unjudged and how many placements they stand
for. Under the real simulator the compression saved more time than it cost,
with its own hashing and isomorphism checks charged against it.

% claim: C18
On hardware the point is not subtle. One plan, deployed at two placements of
itself, meets its latency target on one and misses it sevenfold on the other,
and the planner this work extends cannot tell the two apart. What remains of
the campaign is the inter-node case, and what it waits on is not a missing
disaggregation path but a router: the deployment layer this work extends
serves one engine, and a split serving architecture is two of them with
something in front.
