\section{Provable elimination and an adaptive budget}
\label{sec:search}

% claim: C2
\paragraph{Elimination is a relaxation, or it is not elimination.}
A candidate may be removed only when arithmetic optimistic on every uncertain
term still misses a constraint the specification declares. Five checks
qualify: backend compatibility, device memory, communication latency against
the time-to-first-token target, throughput capacity against a declared goodput
floor, and a cost lower bound against the incumbent. Each publishes its
relaxations---``every KV slot holds a live request'', ``a decode step costs
only the weight sweep'', ``cut capacity is nominal and uncontended''---so a
reader can check that the arithmetic is optimistic rather than merely
plausible. Anything that cannot be shown to be a relaxation is reported as
\texttt{deferred\_heuristic} and not counted among candidates shown
infeasible. The throughput bound runs only when the specification sets a
goodput floor.

\paragraph{The cut bound.}
The graph makes one check possible that an island-level planner cannot
express: the maximum flow between a candidate's device sets, over nominal
capacities minus external reservations, is a ceiling on what its traffic can
achieve. A candidate whose demand exceeds it misses its target under the most
favourable routing. That is a proof relative to the declared cluster
description, not an estimate, and it is as right about the hardware as the
description is.

\paragraph{Ranking is only ranking.}
What survives elimination is ordered by a service-margin estimate: per SLO,
the ratio of what the candidate is estimated to need to what the specification
allows, ranked by the maximum ratio, the binding constraint. The ranker
produces no verdict. Its goodput term once divided by the elimination bound's
optimistic ceiling rather than by a knob-aware throughput estimate, which made
every candidate look comfortable on that axis; the correction was diagnosed on
two fixtures and checked on two it had never seen. Neither correctness number
moves when the ranker changes, which a test asserts: ranking decides what is
evaluated first, not what is eliminated.

\paragraph{An adaptive budget, and what it did not reach.}
The search evaluates representatives in ranked order under a schedule of
increasing $K$, stopping when the budget is exhausted or when no unevaluated
candidate could beat the incumbent, and reports which happened; its recall
against the existing planner's surrogate is reported, with the budgets at
which it does not win, in Section~\ref{sec:eval}. The output names the
representatives never judged and the placements they stand for, as
\texttt{excluded\_by\_scope}: removed by a budget, a property of the search
and not of the hardware. A certificate of optimality, where one is produced,
is bounded by the declared candidate space and the evaluation model: nothing
\emph{evaluated under this model} can do better, not that nothing can.
